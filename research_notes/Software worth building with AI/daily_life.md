## 1. Which specific burdens come up again and again?

### Takeaway
Seven burdens had independent evidence. The best-evidenced one is that people rarely contest a wrong decision (an insurance denial, a benefit refusal) even though a large share of contested decisions are reversed; the next two are not claiming money one is entitled to (cash benefits, and in Germany care benefits) and not understanding official letters. Money management, learning, immigration and tax were not researched (budget ran out), so these notes cannot rank them.

### Cited Findings

**B1. Contesting a wrong decision (insurance denial, medical bill, benefit refusal)**
- CLAIMED. US: fewer than 1% of denied ACA marketplace claims were appealed in 2024 data, and insurers upheld 66% of those appeals (so about a third were reversed) — [KFF, 2024 data, reportedly published March 2026](https://www.kff.org/patient-consumer-protections/claims-denials-and-appeals-in-aca-marketplace-plans-in-2024/)
- CLAIMED. US (2023 survey): 18% of insured adults had a denied claim in the past year; of those, 69% did not know whether they had appeal rights, 86% did not know which agency to call, and 85% filed no formal appeal — [KFF consumer survey brief](https://www.kff.org/affordable-care-act/consumer-survey-highlights-problems-with-denied-health-insurance-claims/)
- CLAIMED. UK: claimants won 67% of PIP (disability benefit) tribunal appeals in the latest quarter reported (27,452 appeals lodged April to June 2026; 37-week wait for a hearing). Other 2025 quarters are reported at 63% — [Benefits and Work, "tribunal backlog soars"](https://benefitsandwork.co.uk/news/tribunal-backlog-soars); [Benefits and Work, "more PIP appeals fail"](https://benefitsandwork.co.uk/news/more-pip-appeals-fail-as-waiting-list-quadruples-in-4-years) (the search summary did not say which of the two pages carries which figure)
- CLAIMED. UK: only 27% of PIP mandatory reconsiderations (the compulsory first-stage review) change the award — [DWP official statistics to July 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/personal-independence-payment-statistics-to-july-2026/personal-independence-payment-statistics-to-july-2026). One advocacy source says 65% of claimants give up after that stage and never reach the tribunal — [Benefits and Work](https://benefitsandwork.co.uk/news/almost-100,000-wait-for-appeal-as-success-rates-fall?rCH=2) (single source, exact page not confirmed)
- CLAIMED. Germany: Jobcenters decided 476,728 objections (Widersprüche) against Bürgergeld notices in 2025; 147,213 were fully or partly upheld, about 30.9% — [buergergeld.org, citing Bundesagentur für Arbeit figures](https://www.buergergeld.org/news/fehlerhafte-buergergeld-bescheide-jeder-dritte-widerspruch-ist-erfolgreich/); [Bundesagentur press release, March 2026](https://www.arbeitsagentur.de/presse/2026-03-buergergeld)
- CLAIMED. What people say (Mumsnet, PIP threads, 2018 to 2026): "it took me hours and hours to think about and then fill in the form"; filling it in for a daughter is "mentally exhausting"; assessment calls of "2 hours 4 minutes" and "nearly 3 hours" — [Mumsnet PIP mental health thread](https://www.mumsnet.com/talk/_chat/5257350-does-anyone-claim-pip-for-mental-health-disorders?page=4); [Mumsnet "filling in PIP form"](https://www.mumsnet.com/talk/am_i_being_unreasonable/4665303-filling-in-pip-form) (which quote sits in which thread was not confirmed)
- CLAIMED. What people ask for: someone else to do the form. "It certainly does help if you can get someone else to complete it for you but not everyone is able to do this" — [Benefits and Work forum](https://benefitsandwork.co.uk/forum/10-dla-esa-queries-results/111956-reminder-text?rCH=2)

**B2. Benefits people are entitled to but do not claim**
- CLAIMED. See section 2 for the figures: £24.1bn a year unclaimed in Great Britain, $58bn a year for US older adults alone, about 60% non-take-up of old-age basic income support in Germany.
- CLAIMED. Stated reasons in the UK report: lack of awareness, complexity and stigma — [Policy in Practice, Missing Out 2025](https://policyinpractice.co.uk/publication/missing-out-2025)

**B3. Care benefits and paperwork when looking after a relative**
- CLAIMED. Germany: a Hochschule Osnabrück study for the social association VdK (56,000 respondents) estimated at least €12bn of care-insurance benefits go unused each year; 62% to 93% of entitled people do not draw a given benefit, and 80% do not draw the €125-a-month relief amount (Entlastungsbetrag). VdK calls it "zu bürokratisch, zu kompliziert und zu niedrig angesetzt" — [epd via evangelisch.de, May 2022](https://www.evangelisch.de/inhalte/200791/08-05-2022/vdk-jaehrlich-verfallen-ansprueche-pflegebeduerftiger-millionenhoehe); [VdK study summary PDF](https://www.vdk.de/assets/bundesverband/dokumente/publikationen_vdk/Pflegestudie_2021/Naechstenpflege_Alleingelassen-und-in-Buerokratie-erstickt_VdK-Pflegestudie_Zentrale-Studienergebnisse_Forderungen.pdf); [final report, Feb 2023](https://www.hs-osnabrueck.de/fileadmin/HSOS/Homepages/DNQP/Dateien/Buescher/VdK-Pflegestudie_Abschlussbericht_Feb_2023.pdf)
- CLAIMED. Same study: when people get advice, use of services rises several-fold (day care from 17% to 83%) — [epd via evangelisch.de](https://www.evangelisch.de/inhalte/200791/08-05-2022/vdk-jaehrlich-verfallen-ansprueche-pflegebeduerftiger-millionenhoehe)
- CLAIMED. US (AgingCare forum): a Medicaid application for a parent described as "Pages and pages of paperwork, continuous letters, and stress"; one renewal "over 30 pages which was less than the 100+ pages in initial application"; a poster reports "SSA does not recognize POA" (power of attorney). Other posters found the form "pretty straightforward" — [AgingCare thread on Medicaid paperwork help](https://www.agingcare.com/questions/amp/166494); [AgingCare thread](https://agingcare.com/questions/amp/479827); [AgingCare forms topic](https://www.agingcare.com/topics/50/forms-documents/questions?page=8) (quote-to-thread mapping not confirmed)
- CLAIMED. US caregivers say they want help "managing complex paperwork" and planning ahead — [AARP/NAC Caregiving in the US 2025](https://www.aarp.org/caregivingintheus2025)

**B4. Official letters people cannot understand**
- CLAIMED. Germany: three in four Germans say they do not understand official German; 41% have paid a late fee because of a misunderstanding; nearly one in four skip their tax return for that reason (Taxfix and Wortliga survey, about 2025, method not seen) — [t-online](https://www.t-online.de/nachrichten/tagesanbruch/id_100424760/rente-steuern-erbe-deutsche-verstehen-amtliche-schreiben-nicht.html)
- CLAIMED. Germany (2008): 86% had difficulty reading letters from offices, authorities, courts or law firms (Allensbach for GfdS) — [GfdS survey PDF](https://gfds.de/wp-content/uploads/2014/08/Umfrage_Rechts-_und_Verwaltungssprache.pdf)

**B5. Admin after a death**
- CLAIMED. An executor "still has to do the work" even with a lawyer: "the lawyer is just going to give you a lot of tasks to do" (planner quoted by NerdWallet, 2021) — [NerdWallet](https://www.nerdwallet.com/article/finance/why-you-may-not-want-to-be-an-executor)
- CLAIMED. UK: 61% of bereaved people had difficulty with at least one practical or administrative task (UK Commission on Bereavement) — [Marie Curie, "Bereavement is everyone's business"](https://mariecurie.org.uk/document/bereavement-is-everyones-business-full-report) (the search summary gave the figure without naming the page; this is the likeliest one). Marie Curie runs a campaign to "Make 'Sadmin' Simple" — [Marie Curie](https://www.mariecurie.org.uk/policy/campaigns/sadmin)
- CLAIMED. Recurring practical traps listed in executor guides: accounts nobody knew existed, each bank wanting its own certified death certificate, and the dead person's mobile number being the second login factor for bank, email and insurer — [Ardent Workshop checklist](https://www.ardentworkshop.com/blog/what-to-do-when-a-parent-dies/); [executor.org](https://executor.org/blog/closing-accounts-deceaseds-name/) (vendor guides, not forum posts)

**B6. The admin work of being a patient**
- CLAIMED. US (2019 survey of 4,155 insured adults): 73% did at least one healthcare admin task in a year (scheduling, getting information, prior authorisation, billing, premium problems); 24.4% delayed or went without care because of one — [Kyle and Frakt, Health Services Research 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8522562); [HealthDay](https://www.healthday.com/healthpro-news/public-health/administrative-tasks-pose-burden-for-patients-2654947814.html)
- CLAIMED. A peer-reviewed paper writes chronic illness up as a job description, including "reviewing EOBs" and appealing denied claims — [PMC, "The work of managing a chronic illness"](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10087252/)

**B7. Understanding one's own records and lab results**
- CLAIMED. 96% of patients surveyed (2023) want immediate access to results even before the clinician has reviewed them; patients are pasting results into chatbots — [NPR, Sept 2025](https://www.npr.org/sections/shots-health-news/2025/09/11/nx-s1-5537067/ai-medicine-privacy-test-results); [KFF Health News](https://kffhealthnews.org/news/article/electronic-medical-records-patients-ai-chatbots-diagnosis-privacy-accuracy/)

### Inferences
- Ranking by evidence gathered this session: B1 and B2 and B3 (strong, multi-country, official statistics) > B4 (strong in Germany, one recent vendor survey) > B6 (one solid academic study) > B5 (real, but nearly all numbers come from companies selling the fix) > B7 (real, but now served by the chatbot vendors themselves).
- B1 to B3 share one shape: money or care exists, a known share of people would get it if they asked properly, and most never ask. That is a completion and follow-through problem more than an information problem.
- B1 is the only burden where both the non-use rate and the success-when-used rate are officially published in three countries.

### Gaps
- No Reddit, Hacker News or AskReddit evidence; no vote or comment counts. The search tool returned error 400 for reddit.com and the proxy returned 403 for old.reddit.com and news.ycombinator.com.
- Not researched at all (search budget exhausted): subscriptions, bill disputes, debt; tutoring and adult learning; immigration forms; tax filing (US and German); SSDI; hospital charity care; dementia-specific logistics; Elterngeld and Pflegegrad applications; r/germany and MoneySavingExpert.
- Forum quotes above came through search summaries; wording and thread attribution need re-checking.

## 3. What do people literally ask for, what exists as of late 2026, and where does it fall short?

### Takeaway
For US insurance denials the field is already crowded (three dedicated tools plus the general chatbots). The thinner spots are outside the US and in follow-through: tools explain or draft one document, and very few carry a person's whole case over months or submit anything.

### Cited Findings

**B1 (US insurance denials and bills)**
- VERIFIED. Fight Health Insurance is free and open source (Apache 2.0 for most code; 156 stars, 47 forks, 1,844 commits on the day read). Its own description: it "drafts an appeal, explains the denial, and points to next steps". The code contains fax and email components, but the page does not say it submits appeals. It uses its own models first and hosted models (Anthropic, DeepInfra, Azure) as backup with user consent — [GitHub repository](https://github.com/totallylegitco/fighthealthinsurance)
- CLAIMED. Claimable: about $50 a case in 2026 ($39.95 plus shipping at its October 2024 launch); files by fax and post and copies state regulators; covers 28 conditions and 90 treatments; $10m raised including from Mark Cuban; says about 3 in 4 users win (self-reported; an 85% figure also circulates); has deals with four drugmakers — [Bloomberg, 22 April 2026](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-04-22/ai-and-mark-cuban-among-startup-s-tools-to-fight-denied-health-care-claims); [Bloomberg Government](https://news.bgov.com/artificial-intelligence/mark-cuban-backed-startup-uses-ai-to-fight-denied-health-claims); [HIT Consultant, 2024 launch](https://hitconsultant.net/2024/10/03/claimable-launches-ai-powered-appeals-platform/); [PYMNTS, 2026](https://www.pymnts.com/artificial-intelligence-2/2026/insurance-denials-meet-their-match-in-ai-powered-appeals/)
- CLAIMED. Counterforce Health: free; drafts appeal letters from an uploaded denial letter and records. Its founder says he does not know its success rate because it does not collect outcomes; one rheumatology clinic reports winning about three-quarters of the time — [Axios Raleigh, Aug 2025](https://www.axios.com/local/raleigh/2025/08/20/using-ai-to-fight-back-against-insurance-denials-counteforce); [AOL](https://www.aol.com/articles/unexpected-way-ai-helping-appeal-094502348.html); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counterforce_Health)
- CLAIMED. Granted Health sells specialised AI for bill negotiation — [Becker's summary of NYT, April 2026](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/healthcare-information-technology/ai/patients-use-chatbots-to-fight-medical-bills-ny-times/)
- Shortfalls. US only; win rates are self-reported and unaudited; Claimable covers a fixed list of conditions; two of three tools draft but leave sending and chasing to the patient (inferred from the descriptions above).

Zurich claims its service saves 148 hours — [Which?](https://www.which.co.uk/news/article/what-financial-admin-do-families-face-after-a-bereavement-ahwm47q2wP6X)
- Shortfall stated by a vendor: AI helps with "research, organization, and deadline-tracking"; tax returns and contested estates still need professionals — [SwiftProbate](https://www.swiftprobate.com/blog/can-ai-help-with-probate)

**B6 and B7 (patient admin, records)**
- CLAIMED. ChatGPT Health (announced 7 Jan 2026, later opened to all logged-in US adults) connects patient portals and Apple Health and answers questions about a user's own lab results; limited outside the US — [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/introducing-chatgpt-health/); [Fierce Healthcare](https://fiercehealthcare.com/ai-and-machine-learning/openai-makes-health-chatgpt-widely-available-moving-deeper-consumer-health)

**Subscriptions (thin evidence)**
- VERIFIED (that the post says this; its numbers are unsourced). A March 2026 vendor post lists what still needs a phone call to cancel: gyms, insurance policies, cable and internet, telecom-bundled streaming, some subscription boxes, warranties. It is an advert for an AI calling agent — [dev.to, Assindo](https://dev.to/assindo/how-to-cancel-any-subscription-without-making-the-call-596f)

### Inferences
- The clearest product gap in this evidence is not "explain this letter" (many tools, plus every chatbot) but "hold my whole file for months and tell me what is due next": B1 outside the US, B3 in Germany, and B2 completion.
- In the US, B1 and B5 already have funded companies; a single engineer would be competing with them on distribution, not capability.
- Benefits tools are being built for caseworkers first. An applicant-side tool is less crowded but meets the political and volume risks in section 4.

### Gaps
- Current state not checked for: Empathy, Atticus, Carefull, Wellthy, ianacare, Rocket Money, DoNotPay's current product, Propel, mRelief, entitledto, Turn2us, any German Pflege or Widerspruch tool, any immigration or tax tool.
- No independent review of any product's failure cases; shortfalls above are mostly read off product descriptions or competitor pages.

## 4. What made this uneconomical before strong AI, and what remains hard?

### Takeaway
The old blocker was that every case is a unique pile of unstructured documents governed by local rules. That part is now tractable. What remains hard is outside the model: authority to act for someone, agencies with no way to receive machine submissions, rule accuracy, liability, and agencies being swamped.

### Cited Findings
- CLAIMED. Volume risk is now documented. "Characterizing Agentic Flooding of Government Services" (Schmitz, Hammond, Chan; AIES 2026) collected 84 cases in 11 jurisdictions: nine in ten showed AI-style submissions, 60% showed higher volume. Risk is highest for "financially attractive, but complex services". One German social-court case had submissions over 4,000 pages. The authors found no evidence yet of autonomous agents operating government portals, and note that countermeasures such as fees hit poorer and less digital users hardest — [arXiv 2608.16603](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.16603); [TechCrunch, 10 Sept 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/10/ai-agents-are-flooding-public-services-with-new-requests/); [Local Government Lawyer](https://localgovernmentlawyer.co.uk/information-law/398-information-law-news/101629-researchers-warn-of-agentic-flooding-of-public-services-with-ai-generated-communications)
- CLAIMED. Accuracy: across more than 22,000 prompts on UK public services, chatbots rarely admitted not knowing and answered even when wrong — [Computer Weekly on the Open Data Institute](https://www.computerweekly.com/news/366638829/Large-language-models-provide-unreliable-answers-about-public-services-Open-Data-Institute-finds). Citizens Advice reports "hallucinations and fictional cases being quoted" — [Citizens Advice, Mills Review submission](https://www.citizensadvice.org.uk/policy/publications/review-into-the-long-term-impact-of-ai-on-retail-financial-services-the-mills-review/)
- CLAIMED. Liability and marketing: the FTC finalised its order against DoNotPay (vote 16 Jan 2025, announced 11 Feb 2025): $193,000 and a ban on claiming it performs like a lawyer without evidence. This was a deceptive-advertising case, not an unauthorised-practice ruling — [FTC order](https://ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/2323042_donotpay_decision_and_order_0.pdf); [Slashdot](https://tech.slashdot.org/story/25/02/11/1932223/)
- CLAIMED. Authority to act: a forum poster reports the US Social Security Administration does not accept a power of attorney; banks need proof of authority before releasing anything about a dead person's account — [AgingCare](https://www.agingcare.com/topics/50/forms-documents/questions?page=8); [executor.org](https://executor.org/blog/closing-accounts-deceaseds-name/)
- CLAIMED. Submission channel: Claimable files by fax and first-class post — [HIT Consultant](https://hitconsultant.net/2024/10/03/claimable-launches-ai-powered-appeals-platform/)
- CLAIMED. Privacy: consumer chatbots sit outside HIPAA, and what patients share could surface in court — [Becker's summary of NYT, April 2026](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/healthcare-information-technology/ai/patients-use-chatbots-to-fight-medical-bills-ny-times/); [An Arm and a Leg](https://armandalegshow.com/first-aid-kit/chatbots-havent-totally-nailed-fighting-medical-bills/) (which of the two carries the point was not confirmed)
- CLAIMED. Jurisdiction traps: ChatGPT told a patient to contact her state insurance commissioner, which was useless because her employer plan was self-insured — [The Star, republishing NYT](https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2026/04/09/patients-are-using-chatbots-to-fight-medical-bills-with-mixed-results)

### Inferences
- A first version that drafts, tracks deadlines and prepares a packet for the person to sign and send avoids the authority and channel problems. It also keeps a human between the tool and the agency, which matters given the flooding evidence.
- Products should avoid any "robot lawyer" style claim; the DoNotPay order is about exactly that.

### Gaps
- Unauthorised-practice-of-law case law (Upsolve in New York, Germany's Rechtsdienstleistungsgesetz) was not retrieved.
- No evidence gathered on how well late-2026 browser agents handle government logins and identity checks.
- No digital-exclusion figures (share of older people offline) retrieved.

## 5. Are people already using general chatbots for these tasks?

### Takeaway
Yes, at very large scale for health insurance and bills in the US, and visibly for UK disability forms. Outcomes are anecdotes with mixed results and no success rate. This shows demand, and it shows that a product which only explains or drafts a single document adds little over a chatbot.

### Cited Findings
- CLAIMED. OpenAI's January 2026 report: more than 40 million people a day ask ChatGPT health questions; 1.6 to 1.9 million messages a week concern health insurance (plans, claims, billing, eligibility), apparently US data — [OpenAI report PDF](https://cdn.openai.com/pdf/2cb29276-68cd-4ec6-a5f4-c01c5e7a36e9/OpenAI-AI-as-a-Healthcare-Ally-Jan-2026.pdf); [Axios](https://axios.com/2026/01/05/chatgpt-openai-health-insurance-aca); [Healthcare Dive gives 1.5 to 2 million](https://www.healthcaredive.com/news/40-million-use-chatgpt-health-questions-openai/808861/)
- CLAIMED. New York Times, April 2026 ("mixed results"): patients use Claude and ChatGPT as a free do-it-yourself route for bills and coverage. Experts who reviewed the chats found the bots "sometimes gave wrong advice, misunderstanding laws or payer intricacies". One patient (Kerr) won although the advice may not have been fully correct. An expert (Broadhurst) said chatbots excel at translating jargon and combing policy documents but lack judgement for complex cases — [The Star republication](https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2026/04/09/patients-are-using-chatbots-to-fight-medical-bills-with-mixed-results); [Becker's](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/healthcare-information-technology/ai/patients-use-chatbots-to-fight-medical-bills-ny-times/); [An Arm and a Leg](https://armandalegshow.com/first-aid-kit/chatbots-havent-totally-nailed-fighting-medical-bills/)
- CLAIMED. Individual stories: a $195,000 hospital bill cut to $33,000 after a letter written with Claude and cross-checked with ChatGPT (originates from a social media post, unverified) — [Fox News](https://www.foxnews.com/tech/163k-fake-medical-bill-charges-ai-uncovers-you); a Business Insider writer's dental appeal was denied but she never had to pay — [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/sectors/healthcare/articles/used-ai-dispute-1-200-090501187.html); "AI vs. AI" coverage of patients against insurer algorithms — [Stateline, Nov 2025](https://stateline.org/2025/11/20/patients-deploy-bots-to-battle-health-insurers-that-deny-care/)
- CLAIMED. Lab results: three chatbots answered patient questions about a clinical note well, and accuracy improved when asked one question at a time; in a small study of ChatGPT radiology summaries (118 patients) most found them clearer but some were more confused — [KFF Health News](https://kffhealthnews.org/news/article/electronic-medical-records-patients-ai-chatbots-diagnosis-privacy-accuracy/); [NPR](https://www.npr.org/sections/shots-health-news/2025/09/11/nx-s1-5537067/ai-medicine-privacy-test-results)
- CLAIMED. Harm side: a Uswitch survey claims more than five million Britons have been harmed by wrong chatbot advice — [Uswitch, Aug 2026](https://www.uswitch.com/media-centre/2026/08/five-million-brits-harmed-by-wrong-ai-advice-from-lost-cash-to-health-scares/) (self-reported survey by a comparison site)

### Inferences
- A dedicated product is **not** needed for: explaining a letter, a bill or a lab result, or drafting one appeal. Chatbots do this now and the vendors are building it in (ChatGPT Health).
- A dedicated product **may** be needed for what the chat format does badly: remembering a case over months, knowing the plan type or jurisdiction before advising (the self-insured trap), tracking deadlines, checking its own citations, and producing the exact form or packet. That is my inference from the failure cases above; no study tests it.

### Gaps
- No study measuring success rates of chatbot-written appeals against a control. No German survey on chatbot use for official letters. No Anthropic or OpenAI usage breakdown for benefits, tax or immigration tasks.

## 6. Where is demand thinner than it looks?

### Takeaway
Three places: stand-alone personal health records, caregiver coordination apps, and (on this evidence) paid death-admin tools for small estates. In each, people describe the pain but the product asks them to do ongoing data entry or pay up front.

### Cited Findings
- CLAIMED. Google Health closed on 1 January 2012 for lack of adoption. An IDC survey then found about 7% had ever used a personal health record; healthy people use them "very sporadically"; importing data was hard — [Computerworld, 2011](https://www.computerworld.com/article/2509515/why-google-health-failed--too-little--too-soon.html); [MobiHealthNews](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/news/official-google-health-shuts-down-because-it-couldnt-scale-adoption)
- VERIFIED. Caregiver coordination apps have very small footprints: Caring Village 36 App Store ratings; CareCoordinate too few to show — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1093814557); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6764144454)
- CLAIMED. An editor who gave his digital chores to an AI agent reported he ended up maintaining the agent — [Every](https://every.to/p/i-hired-an-ai-to-do-my-chores-now-i-maintain-the-ai) (paywalled; opening only)
- CLAIMED. A 2015 startup, Airpaper, offered to do paperwork and follow-up calls for $5 (first service: cancelling Comcast) — [Marketplace, 2015](https://marketplace.org/2015/10/05/dealing-red-tape-theres-app). Its later fate was not checked.
- CLAIMED. For B1 the scale number can mislead: fewer than 1% appeal, but KFF notes its survey "cannot tell how often claims denials are incorrect", and 66% of appeals fail — [KFF](https://www.kff.org/affordable-care-act/consumer-survey-highlights-problems-with-denied-health-insurance-claims/)

### Inferences
- Tools that need the user to keep a record up to date fail; tools triggered by an event with money attached (a denial letter, a refusal, a death) have a natural moment of use. B1, B2 and B3 are event-triggered; caregiver coordination and health records are not.
- B5's hour counts are vendor numbers and the US market has several funded entrants charging thousands of dollars; demand for a cheap self-serve tool is unproven here.
- Rejected as first versions, with reasons:
  - Acting inside agency or bank portals on someone's behalf: needs identity and legal authority the builder cannot obtain.
  - Automated bulk submission to agencies: feeds the documented flooding problem and invites fees or blocks.
  - A personal health record or finance aggregator as the core product: prior adoption failures, and dependence on data connections one engineer cannot secure outside the US.
  - Anything presenting itself as a lawyer substitute: the DoNotPay order.
  - Subscription cancelling by AI phone agent: only vendor evidence found, and it borders on automated calling.
- Not rejected, simply unevidenced: tutoring and language learning, immigration forms, tax filing, debt and bill disputes.

### Gaps
- No evidence on Mint's closure or finance-aggregator engagement, no conversion data for benefits screeners (how many who are told they qualify go on to claim), no churn data for any tool named here.
