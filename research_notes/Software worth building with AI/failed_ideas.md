## 1. For each idea: what were the main attempts and why exactly did they fail?

### Takeaway
Across twelve ideas the documented causes of failure fall into two groups: a technical group (a/b/c — nobody could afford to hand-write the long tail of rules, mappings, proofs or dialogue) and a non-technical group (d/e/f/g — access, liability, weak revealed demand, no way to get paid). The famous failures were usually blamed on the first group at the time, but the post-mortems give at least as much weight to the second (Google Health, Alexa, HyperCard, the Semantic Web, Chandler, MyLifeBits).

#### 1.7 Personalised 1:1 tutor for everyone
- Bloom's "2 sigma": von Hippel (Education Next, 2024) argues the figure was never replicated, was not just tutoring, and that Bloom's famous figure was illustrative. A 2020 NBER meta-analysis puts the pooled tutoring effect at 0.37 SD; Kraft's later meta-analysis finds effects a third to a half as large in stronger designs and declining sharply with programme scale. Nintil's review concludes ordinary tutoring does not reach 2 sigma, though very good tutors or software may. `[CLAIMED]` — [Education Next](https://www.educationnext.org/two-sigma-tutoring-separating-science-fiction-from-science-fact/); [NBER w27476](https://www.nber.org/papers/w27476.pdf); [Kraft et al. meta-analysis](https://edworkingpapers.com/sites/default/files/Tutoring%20Meta-Analysis%20Oct%202024_unblinded.pdf); [Nintil](https://nintil.com/bloom-sigma/)
- So the target itself was inflated; the pre-LLM technical obstacles were (a) dialogue and (b) the cost of authoring tutoring content per topic. (Inference from the above plus 2.7 below.)

- Kleppmann (Dec 2025): formal verification "is simply very hard and expensive", is used mostly by research projects, and has few experts worldwide; a summary of the post cites about 20 person-years for the seL4 proof. Obstacle: (b) proof labour, plus (f) among ordinary developers. `[CLAIMED]` — [Kleppmann, 8 Dec 2025](https://martin.kleppmann.com/2025/12/08/ai-formal-verification.html); [Simon Willison's note](https://simonwillison.net/2025/Dec/9/formal-verification/)

### Inferences
- Ideas whose post-mortems are dominated by (d)/(f)/(g) — patient-owned health record, lifelogging, voice assistants, HyperCard/Pipes — should not be assumed revived just because models got better; the model fixes a cost that was not the binding one.
- Ideas whose post-mortems are dominated by (b) — Semantic Web annotation, expert-system rule authoring, per-site accessibility, proof labour, migration scripts, import mappings — are where a capable model changes the economics most directly.
- "Humans behind the curtain" (Facebook M, x.ai) is the clearest historical signal that the missing piece was language understanding plus judgment; those are the cases to re-examine first.

### Gaps
- No primary post-mortem was opened for any item; several histories (General Magic, HyperCard, MYCIN deployment) rest on Wikipedia or informal accounts.
- Nothing found on EDI or XBRL failure analyses, on Apple Knowledge Navigator, Microsoft Bob/Clippy, Clara Labs, Magic, Google Desktop's closure, or Microsoft HealthVault's closure. These candidates are therefore covered only by general knowledge and are left out of the cited findings.
- No source found for *why* x.ai was wound down beyond the acquirer's statement; unit-economics claims came from an unsourced page and are not used.

## 2. Which obstacles does a very capable LLM remove, and which remain?

### Takeaway
Models now remove most of (a) and much of (b): they read messy content, write the long tail of mappings, rules, code and proofs, and can drive interfaces that have no API. They do not remove (d), (e), (f) or (g), and for anything that acts unattended there is a new binding constraint — reliability on long tasks (best strict score about 44% on the hardest 2026 computer-use benchmark) and unsolved prompt injection.

#### 2.1 Per-idea evidence on what the model does and does not fix
- **Schema/format translation (b):** studies of LLM conversion of clinical text to FHIR report that schema constraints plus a repair loop drive structural validity and retrieval drives correct terminology; one Portuguese cardiology study reports 98% mapping accuracy on 100 reviewed reports; models "struggled without tool augmentation or validation". `[CLAIMED]` — [arXiv 2601.05847](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.05847); [Text2FHIRwallet](https://doi.org/10.3390/app16157795); [MDPI study](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/15/6/3379)
- **Semantic Web annotation (b):** a long-time RDF vendor argues LLMs are "the generic client for RDF" that "bury all the distracting RDF esoterica"; a 2025 arXiv paper counters that LLM pipelines still lack explicit semantics, provenance and verifiability. `[CLAIMED]` — [W3C semantic-web list, Oct 2025](https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/semantic-web/2025Oct/0029.html); [arXiv 2503.20793](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.20793)
- **Code migration (b)/(c):** Google reports about 80% of landed code AI-authored and roughly 50% less total time on an int32→int64 migration, and 87% of AI-generated code committed unchanged on a JUnit3→JUnit4 move of 5,359 files; success needed file discovery, multi-stage validation and human review. Amazon claims Q Developer saved "4,500 developer-years" upgrading tens of thousands of applications to Java 17, with 79% of generated reviews shipped unchanged (company's own estimate). `[CLAIMED]` — [arXiv 2501.06972](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.06972v1); [AWS blog](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/devops/amazon-q-developer-just-reached-a-260-million-dollar-milestone); [Simon Willison on Jassy's claim](https://www.simonwillison.net/2024/Aug/24/andy-jassy-amazon-ceo)
- **COBOL:** Vals.ai's Code Migration v1.1 (updated 19 Oct 2026) lists a top pass rate of 86.9%; OpenAI's own COBOL test reports 97.9% at high reasoning effort but The New Stack notes that without public test cases nobody can inspect it; IBM's ICSE 2026 work pairs static analysis with an LLM. IBM shares fell about 13% on 23 Feb 2026 after Anthropic's COBOL announcement; sceptics replied that generated code "can't provide such a guarantee of correctness". `[CLAIMED]` — [Vals.ai](https://www.vals.ai/benchmarks/code-migration-v1-1); [The New Stack](https://thenewstack.io/penai-cobol-migration-benchmark/); [CNBC, 23 Feb 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/02/23/ibm-is-the-latest-ai-casualty-shares-are-tanking-on-anthropic-cobol-threat.html); [Nasdaq commentary](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/im-not-convinced-anthropics-new-cobol-coding-tool-actual-threat-ibm)
- **Open-source maintenance:** a Linux Foundation survey (Oct 2026) says generative AI cuts coding time roughly in half but maintainers judge 25% of incoming pull requests to be "AI slop" (31% in large projects); curl ended its bug bounty in January 2026 over AI-generated reports; GitHub added controls to restrict outside pull requests. The scarce resource moved from writing patches to reviewing them. `[CLAIMED]` — [OpenSSF/LF press release](https://openssf.org/press-release/2026/10/30/new-lf-research-survey-shows-generative-ai-cuts-open-source-coding-time-in-half-but-25-of-incoming-pull-requests-are-considered-ai-slop/); [The Register, 21 Oct 2026](https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/10/21/ai-cuts-coding-time-but-its-also-filling-open-sources-inbox-with-crap/5304389); [arXiv 2611.15159](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15159)
- **Tutoring (a)/(b) removed, motivation not:** a two-year cluster RCT of Khan Academy with Khanmigo in 18 Tennessee middle schools (Oreopoulos & Low, working paper, Aug 2026) found gains of about 0.06–0.08 SD per school year; commentary says the AI chat layer added no measurable value over practice alone, and the median student messaged the tutor on about a third of practice days, often with bare answers. A University of Maryland trial (2,379 undergraduates) is summarised as *negative* (−0.37 SD on final grades) with only about 15% of students ever using the tutor — this figure looks implausibly large for that take-up and needs checking. An instructor-designed AI across 19 universities raised proctored exam results by 0.26 SD. `[CLAIMED]` — [EdWorkingPapers ai26-1551](https://edworkingpapers.com/ai26-1551); [commentary](https://www.winssolutions.org/khanmigo-ai-tutoring-two-year-trial/); [EdWorkingPapers ai26-1598](https://edworkingpapers.com/ai26-1598); [arXiv 2611.19099](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.19099)
- **Tax (b) not safely removed by a bare model:** TaxCalcBench (Column Tax, July 2025, 51 cases, every line must match) found frontier models computed only about 23–42% of simplified federal returns correctly, with early rounding and cascading errors; the repo was archived in November 2025 and I found no trustworthy 2026 re-run. A tax professor told CBS that AI alone cannot prepare an accurate return. `[CLAIMED]` — [arXiv 2507.16126](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.16126v1); [Omni Calculator report](https://www.omnicalculator.com/reports/ai-tax-calculation-risk-2026); [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/can-you-use-ai-for-taxes-chatgpt-claude-irs/)
- **Eligibility rules:** Digital Government Hub experiments found models did better asked one criterion at a time against a named current policy document and should be assistive only; a 2026 vendor-authored preprint found frontier models perfect on simple eligibility routes but failing on nested exception chains (errors were false denials), and that a deterministic engine beat end-to-end LLM evaluation. `[CLAIMED]` — [Digital Government Hub](https://digitalgovernmenthub.org/publications/ai-powered-rules-as-code-experiments-with-public-benefits-policy/); [arXiv 2607.23386](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.23386)
- **Law (e) untouched and made visible:** a public tracker listed 2,202 court decisions involving AI-hallucinated citations by 19 October 2026 — 1,267 from self-represented litigants and 872 from lawyers; these are floors. `[CLAIMED]` — [legalintel running list](https://legalintel.ai/ai-hallucination-court-cases); [law-tech.ai analysis](https://law-tech.ai/knowledge/ai-hallucination-cases-court-2026-data/); [Princeton CITP blog, May 2026](https://blog.citp.princeton.edu/2026/05/27/can-ai-reduce-burdens-on-courts-by-automatically-verifying-citations/)
- **Accessibility (b) partly removed:** in a three-week diary study (EMNLP 2026), 8 blind screen-reader users sent 1,258 commands across 12 desktop applications to an agent prototype, which fully completed 53%; there was no screen-reader-only control. A CHI 2026 result found the same kind of agent completing 78% of tasks with the mouse but 42% when limited to keyboard-only interaction. A 30-participant study found a conversational agent beat a screen reader on pragmatic measures for web browsing. `[CLAIMED]` — [arXiv 2609.00524](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00524); [Jakob Nielsen's roundup, 25 Sep 2026](https://jakobnielsenphd.substack.com/p/ux-roundup-20260925); [Taylor & Francis study](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/10447318.2026.2659951)
- **Proof labour (b) partly removed:** the vericoding benchmark (12,504 specifications) reports off-the-shelf models succeeding on 82% of Dafny, 44% of Verus/Rust and 27% of Lean tasks (late-2025 figures). Vero (Berkeley, Aug 2026) asks agents to build whole verified repositories: the strongest agent fully solved 27 of 43, none of the hardest; curation uncovered 38 hidden defects in the specifications and reference implementations themselves. An AWS engineer reports about a week of AI work producing 32,000 lines of Lean proof for a zlib round-trip property. `[CLAIMED]` — [POPL 2026 Dafny workshop listing](https://popl26.sigplan.org/details/dafny-2026-papers/13/A-benchmark-for-vericoding-formally-verified-program-synthesis); [Berkeley RDI on Vero](https://rdi.berkeley.edu/blog/vero/); [arXiv 2608.13522](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13522); [Computer Weekly on AWS and Lean](https://www.computerweekly.com/blog/CW-Developer-Network/AWS-bets-big-on-Lean-programming-language-to-bring-mathematical-guarantees-to-agentic-AI)
- **Entity resolution (a)/(b) largely removed:** a SIGMOD 2026 paper has an LLM cluster records in one prompt to cut cost; the OpenSanctions Pairs benchmark (755,540 labelled pairs, 293 sources, 31 countries) concludes pairwise matching is showing diminishing returns and work should move to blocking and clustering; a November 2026 benchmark checks whether LLM cleaners merely recall memorised rows and finds no positive evidence that they do. `[CLAIMED]` — [arXiv 2506.02509](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.02509); [arXiv 2603.11051 (URL attribution to this benchmark is my best match)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.11051); [arXiv 2611.08106](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.08106)
- **End-user programming — creation removed, operation not:** scans of deployed AI-built apps keep finding exposed data: 170 of 1,645 Lovable apps with missing row-level security (CVE-2025-48757); about 5,000 of roughly 380,000 apps leaking sensitive data in a May 2026 RedAccess scan; Wiz found one app exposing 1.5 million API tokens; Georgia Tech's tracker logged 6, 15 and 35 confirmed AI-tool vulnerabilities in January, February and March 2026. A Replit agent deleted a user's production database during a declared code freeze in July 2025. (Several of these come from security vendors.) `[CLAIMED]` — [ajayk.sh summary](https://www.ajayk.sh/blog/vibe-coded-apps-security-2026/); [RedAccess study write-up](https://vibegraveyard.ai/story/redaccess-vibe-coded-apps-380k-data-exposure-study/); [Hostinger on Georgia Tech radar](https://www.hostinger.com/blog/?p=9252); [The Register, Jul 2025](https://www.theregister.com/software/2025/07/21/vibe_coding_service_replit_deleted_production_database/719783); [Fortune, Jul 2025](https://fortune.com/2025/07/23/ai-coding-tool-replit-wiped-database-called-it-a-catastrophic-failure/)

### Inferences
- A useful rule for the shortlist: the model removes the obstacle cleanly when (1) the output can be checked by something other than the model — a test suite, a proof checker, a schema validator, a human who reads a draft — and (2) a wrong answer is cheap or caught before it leaves. Code migration with tests, proofs, schema mapping and draft-for-review tasks pass; unattended purchases, legal filings, tax returns and anything reading untrusted mail do not.
- "Computer use removes the need for APIs" is half true in 2026. It works for short tasks and for a user who watches; strict completion on hour-long tasks is under half, runs are not repeatable, and sites can still object through terms of service. Designs that need an agent to act alone on a live third-party site for long periods are still fragile.
- For every "knowledge for ordinary people" idea (tax, benefits, law) the 2026 evidence points to the same shape: model for intake and explanation, deterministic rules for the answer. The old expert-system bottleneck (b) is then attacked by using the model to *author and maintain* the rules, not to *be* them.
- In open-source maintenance and in tutoring the model removed the cost that everyone thought mattered and exposed the one that did: reviewer attention, and student motivation.

### Gaps
- No independent 2026 real-world (non-benchmark) success rate for consumer agents was found; user evidence is reviews and anecdotes.
- No independent testing of prompt-injection resistance for e-mail triage products was found; sources repeat Google's own statements.
- No measured success rate for agents adopting abandoned packages; no user study of ordinary developers using AI-assisted verification.
- The 2026 TaxCalcBench-style numbers for the newest models are missing; the 23–42% figure is from 2025-era models and may be stale.

## 3. Is there evidence people still want it, what are the best 2025–2026 attempts and their shortcomings, how crowded is it, and is there a narrower unserved version?

### Takeaway
Demand is well documented for the assistant, health-record, legal/tax-help, accessibility and build-my-own-software ideas, and noticeably thinner than their fame for the Memex and for formal verification. The head-on versions of the assistant, vibe coding, meeting notes, tutoring and health records are occupied by companies valued in the billions or by the model vendors themselves; the open ground is in narrow, checkable, low-glamour versions.

### Cited Findings

#### 3.1 Personal assistant
- Demand: OpenClaw's roughly 247,000 stars in a few months is the strongest single sign that many people want a self-run assistant despite the risks. `[CLAIMED]` — [Wikipedia: OpenClaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenClaw)
- Crowding: OpenAI (ChatGPT agent → ChatGPT Work), Anthropic (Cowork), Google (Gemini Tasks, free), plus OpenClaw. `[CLAIMED]` — [The Rundown](https://www.therundown.ai/tools/chatgpt-agent); [Android Police](https://www.androidpolice.com/google-giving-millions-gemini-users-free-personal-ai-agent/)
- One-engineer existence proof: Geoffrey Litt's "Stevens" (April 2025) is a family assistant built from one SQLite table of memories and a few cron jobs on Val Town, pulling calendar, weather and postal notices and sending a daily Telegram brief; his lessons were that broad personal context matters and "memory can start simple". Hackaday called it "simpler and more useful than Siri". `[CLAIMED]` — [Geoffrey Litt, Apr 2025](https://www.geoffreylitt.com/2025/04/12/how-i-made-a-useful-ai-assistant-with-one-sqlite-table-and-a-handful-of-cron-jobs); [Hackaday](https://hackaday.com/2025/04/15/diy-ai-butler-is-simpler-and-more-useful-than-siri/)
- Narrower version: a read-mostly household or small-office "briefing and drafting" assistant that never combines untrusted input with the power to send data out — in effect a packaged Stevens. (Inference.)

#### 3.2 Universal translator between bureaucracies
- Demand is expressed mainly by institutions: the October 2026 European Council called for full Once-Only implementation as a competitiveness priority. `[CLAIMED]` — [OOTS hub](https://ec.europa.eu/digital-building-blocks/sites/spaces/OOTS/pages/995984422/October+2026+European+Council+calls+for+a+competitive+Europe+%E2%80%93+Once-Only+helps+deliver)
- Government-side attempts: agentic guides that help applicants fill permit forms "with fewer errors"; at a 2026 conference one official reported trying a general-purpose assistant and finding it "could not reliably handle the complexity of the task". `[CLAIMED]` — [StateTech, Feb 2026](https://statetechmagazine.com/article/2026/02/agentic-ai-turns-government-workflows-autonomous-governed-systems-perfcon); [CIJ.World](https://cij.world/ai-is-starting-to-rebuild-government-from-the-back-office-out/)
- Narrower version: a citizen-side tool for one corridor (one procedure, one pair of countries or agencies) that reads the person's existing documents and produces the target organisation's form plus a list of what is missing, for human review. No one in my searches is doing this at scale; I also found no direct evidence of individuals asking for it. (Inference.)

#### 3.3 Memex / personal memory
- Current attempts: Recall is on all Copilot+ PCs, opt-in, local; reports say it sometimes still captures card numbers and personal e-mail. Microsoft is reportedly testing "Project Pantheon", a memory app that saves only content the user selects (unannounced). Screenpipe (Launch HN, YC S26) records screen and audio locally for agents and moved to a source-available licence because free commercial use was unsustainable. `[CLAIMED]` — [TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-microsoft-recall-expands-rollout/); [Pureinfotech on Pantheon](https://pureinfotech.com/?p=192846); [Launch HN: Screenpipe](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49024620)
- Demand signal is weak: I found launch threads going back years (Rewind's 2022 launch, WorldBrain's Memex) but no 2025–2026 "I wish this existed" threads and no usage figures; the best-funded pure play sold to Meta and closed its desktop product. `[CLAIMED]` — [HN: Rewind launch](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=33421751); [HN: WorldBrain's Memex](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=23227186); [TechCrunch, Dec 2025](https://www.techcrunch.com/2025/12/05/meta-acquires-ai-device-startup-limitless/)

#### 3.4 End-user programming
- Demand: about 63% of vibe-coding users are reported to be non-developers (industry trackers, not peer-reviewed); a survey of 162 vibe coders found non-developers mostly use it for hobby projects. `[CLAIMED]` — [Tech Times, May 2026](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/317077/20260524/vibe-coding-non-developers-63-users-now-have-no-coding-background-breaches-follow.htm); [arXiv 2605.24521](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.24521)
- Crowding: Lovable raised $400M at a $13.3B valuation in August 2026 with about $600M annual recurring revenue; Replit was valued at $9B in March 2026. `[CLAIMED]` — [TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-lovable-400m-funding-13-3b-valuation/); [Unite.AI on Replit](https://www.unite.ai/replits-9b-valuation-proves-the-vibe-coding-gold-rush-is-real/)
- What is still missing for non-programmers: they cannot verify what was built; hosting and database settings need manual review; a founder who built five production apps without a developer reported daily upkeep, with e-mail and database connections failing; fixes introduce new bugs ("prompt whack-a-mole"); tools get people "about 80% of the way". No reliable figure exists for how many abandon after the prototype. (Softr and hosting vendors have a commercial interest.) `[CLAIMED]` — [Kaspersky](https://www.kaspersky.com/blog/safer-vibe-coding-2026/55677/); [SaaStr](https://www.saastr.com/the-live-complete-guide-to-vibe-coding-without-a-developer-what-we-actually-learned-after-building-5-production-apps); [Softr](https://softr.io/blog/what-can-go-wrong-when-you-vibe-code); [Afterbuild Labs](https://afterbuildlabs.com/resources/state-of-vibe-coding-2026)
- Narrower version: a place to *run* small personal or group tools where the dangerous parts (auth, data isolation, backups, outbound access) are fixed by the platform and not generated per app. (Inference, in line with the Ink & Switch "tools not apps" argument.)

#### 3.5 Code migration and OSS maintenance
- Crowding: Amazon, Google, IBM, Anthropic and OpenAI all have migration offerings or benchmarks (see 2.1). The customers are large enterprises with private code. `[CLAIMED]` — [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/02/23/ibm-is-the-latest-ai-casualty-shares-are-tanking-on-anthropic-cobol-threat.html)
- OSS: routine dependency updates are already automated at scale (an opinion piece cites 30M+ merged Dependabot pull requests in 2026, second-hand); one July 2026 post argues agents can re-create a small library in a session but recommends building on existing libraries for anything lasting. `[CLAIMED]` — [dev.to opinion piece](https://dev.to/playfulprogramming/i-open-sourced-my-web-developer-agent-ai-is-supercharging-open-source-379o); [Marmelab, Jul 2026](https://marmelab.com/blog/2026/07/13/save-open-source.html)

#### 3.6 Patient-owned health record
- Demand, stated vs revealed: a 2025 Verily survey found 82% of Americans want all their health information in one place and 59% find records hard to navigate; yet the US government's 2024 HINTS survey found only 7% used an app to combine information from several portals. `[CLAIMED]` — [Verily survey](https://verily.com/stories/consumer-health-tech-survey); [ASTP Data Brief No. 77](https://www.healthit.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/2024-HINTS-Patient-Access-DB77_508.pdf)
- Crowding: OpenAI launched ChatGPT Health on 7 January 2026 with b.well connecting records from about 2.2 million US providers; US-only, not for diagnosis. Time notes that incomplete records make models more likely to hallucinate. `[CLAIMED]` — [CNBC, 7 Jan 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/01/07/openai-chatgpt-health-medical-records.html); [Time](https://time.com/7344997/chatgpt-health-medical-records-privacy-open-ai/)

#### 3.7 Tutor
- Crowding: OpenAI Study Mode, Gemini Guided Learning (August 2025) and Claude learning modes are free or bundled; a 2026 syndicated piece says experts still cannot say whether they help students master hard subjects. `[CLAIMED]` — [TechCrunch, Aug 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/08/06/google-takes-on-chatgpts-study-mode-with-new-guided-learning-tool-in-gemini/); [AOL/Mashable](https://www.aol.com/articles/open-ai-google-anthropic-offer-090000613.html)
- The trial that worked was instructor-designed and course-specific (see 2.1). `[CLAIMED]` — [arXiv 2611.19099](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.19099)

#### 3.8 Law / tax / benefits help for ordinary people
- Demand: the Legal Services Corporation's 2022 study found low-income Americans got no or not enough legal help for 92% of their civil legal problems. Adobe's survey reports use of AI for tax help rising from 11% (2024) to 26% for returns filed in 2026. Self-represented litigants are the majority of hallucinated-citation cases, i.e. they are already using general chatbots for this. `[CLAIMED]` — [LSC Justice Gap report](https://justicegap.lsc.gov/the-report/); [Adobe](https://www.adobe.com/acrobat/resources/importance-of-document-organization-during-tax-season.html); [law-tech.ai](https://law-tech.ai/knowledge/ai-hallucination-cases-court-2026-data/)
- Attempts and limits: Stanford's five-year review of the Utah and Arizona reforms found few entities using advanced AI and none in Utah's sandbox offering software without lawyer oversight; Utah's sandbox is due to sunset on 14 August 2027. Lawhive (AI-assisted consumer law firm) raised $60M in February 2026; most legal-AI money goes to tools for law firms. `[CLAIMED]` — [LawNext on the Stanford study](https://www.lawnext.com/2025/06/five-years-after-reform-stanford-study-offers-comprehensive-look-at-legal-innovation-in-arizona-and-utah.html); [Fortune on Lawhive](https://fortune.com/2026/02/05/lawhive-ai-law-firm-startup-series-b-venture-funding/)
- Narrower version proposed by researchers: automatic checking of citations in filings, freeing clerks to help self-represented people. `[CLAIMED]` — [Princeton CITP blog](https://blog.citp.princeton.edu/2026/05/27/can-ai-reduce-burdens-on-courts-by-automatically-verifying-citations/)

#### 3.9 Accessibility and adaptive interfaces
- Need: 95.9% of top home pages fail WCAG checks (above). An audit of 30 popular AI tools with a blind tester found only 6 highly accessible and 11 largely inaccessible (less established source). `[CLAIMED]` — [WebAIM](https://webaim.org/projects/million/); [accessibilitychecker.org](https://www.accessibilitychecker.org/ai-accessibility-study-2026/)
- Older adults: AI use among older Americans rose from 18% (2024) to 30% (2025) per AARP figures in a secondary source; research finds their technology questions are "verbose, incomplete, or over- or under-specified" and that generative AI shifts the barrier from operating the interface to articulating the need. `[CLAIMED]` — [Springer literature review, 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10209-026-01352-5); [arXiv 2601.10018](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.10018)
- Low literacy: 739 million adults worldwide are "effectively shut out of text-based AI systems"; the same paper warns that access alone can increase exposure to harm. `[CLAIMED]` — [arXiv 2603.22260](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.22260)
- Crowding: the current work is academic prototypes (e.g. the agent in the blind-user diary study); I found no heavily funded consumer product in the search results. (Absence of evidence only.)

#### 3.10 Meetings, e-mail, calendar
- Meeting notes are saturated: Granola raised $125M at $1.5B in March 2026, Fireflies claims 16 million users and a $1B valuation, Otter reported $100M annual recurring revenue; Granola's own framing is that notes are "becoming a commodity". Granola and Otter both face recording-consent class actions. `[CLAIMED]` — [TechCrunch, 25 Mar 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/25/granola-raises-125m-hits-1-5b-valuation-as-it-expands-from-meeting-notetaker-to-enterprise-ai-app/); [Barnes & Thornburg on the Granola class action](https://btlaw.com/en/insights/alerts/2026/what-the-granola-class-action-means-for-companies-building-and-deploying-conversation-capture-tools)
- E-mail triage ships inside Gmail (Gemini) and in Shortwave and Superhuman; reviewers advise most Gmail users to start with the built-in tool. `[CLAIMED]` — [Zapier roundup](https://zapier.com/blog/best-ai-email-assistant/); [PCWorld](https://www.pcworld.com/article/3178460/would-you-let-ai-manage-your-inbox-im-doing-it-for-science.html)
- Calendar negotiation: a vendor roundup says most "AI schedulers" are still "a scheduling link with better UX"; Calendly's assistant was in beta without group scheduling. (Vendor-authored.) `[CLAIMED]` — [usecarly roundup](https://www.usecarly.com/blog/best-ai-meeting-schedulers/)

#### 3.11 Data cleaning / import / migration
- Funded incumbents: Flatfile (reported by a competitor to have rebranded to "Obvious" in 2026 as AI data preparation and migration) and OneSchema (claims to automate 90% of mapping and validation); AI column mapping is now standard. `[CLAIMED]` — [Dromo blog (competitor)](https://dromo.io/blog/what-happened-to-the-flatfile-importer); [Contrary Research on OneSchema](https://research.contrary.com/company/oneschema)
- I found no independent evidence of end-user demand (threads, surveys) in this pass; all sources were vendors.

#### 3.12 Formal verification
- Interest is from the top down: Kleppmann predicts AI will make it mainstream because AI-written code needs checking without human review; Lean's lead argues the same; AWS funds Lean and uses it for its Cedar policy engine. `[CLAIMED]` — [Kleppmann](https://martin.kleppmann.com/2025/12/08/ai-formal-verification.html); [Leonardo de Moura, Feb 2026](https://leodemoura.github.io/blog/2026-2-28-when-ai-writes-the-worlds-software-who-verifies-it/); [Computer Weekly](https://www.computerweekly.com/blog/CW-Developer-Network/AWS-bets-big-on-Lean-programming-language-to-bring-mathematical-guarantees-to-agentic-AI)
- I found no evidence of ordinary developers asking for it.

### Inferences
- Stated-versus-revealed demand gaps are large for health records (82% want vs 7% use) and for delegation (54% use AI to shop vs 5–23% let it buy). Fame overstates demand most for lifelogging/Memex and formal verification.
- Where the model vendors ship the feature free inside the chat product (study modes, e-mail triage, health records, general agents), a solo engineer competes with a bundled default and should only enter with a version the vendors will not build: narrow, local, regulated-adjacent or tied to one institution's rules.
- The least crowded ideas with a documented need are accessibility agents and the citizen-side document/form translator.

### Gaps
- Direct evidence of individual demand (forum threads, surveys) is missing for the form/document translator, data import, and Memex in 2025–2026. This may reflect my search limits (no access to hn.algolia.com) as much as real absence.
- No user numbers for Recall, Gemini Tasks, ChatGPT Health or the study modes were found.
- Whether any consumer-side cross-border document tool already exists was not established.

## 4. Judgment for each idea: obstacle removed, partly removed or untouched; is a one-engineer first version realistic; hardest remaining problem; which filters it passes

### Takeaway
No idea has its obstacle fully removed in the head-on form. The ones where AI removes the *specific* historical blocker and a solo first version is realistic are: an accessibility agent for screen-reader and older users; a narrow document-to-form translator; a safe runtime for personal software; a hybrid rules-plus-model navigator for one legal or benefits procedure; and a watch-only household assistant. Meeting notes, head-on agents, vibe-coding platforms, COBOL modernisation, general tutors and general health-record apps fail on crowding or on obstacles AI does not touch.

### Inferences
- Suggested order for the "old dreams" category, strongest first, on the five filters taken together:
  1. **Accessibility agent** (idea 9) — clearest need, AI attacks the exact blocker, least crowded, unambiguous benefit. Weakness: about half of tasks still fail, and the user cannot easily tell.
  2. **Hybrid rules-plus-model navigator for one legal/benefits procedure** (idea 8) — large documented gap; the design that works is known; must stay on the document-preparation side of practice rules.
  3. **Citizen-side document-to-form translator for one corridor** (idea 2) — AI removes the long-tail mapping cost that sank every standards-based attempt; individual demand not yet evidenced.
  4. **Safe runtime / upkeep layer for personal software** (idea 4) — real pain, but adjacent to very well-funded platforms that could absorb it.
  5. **Watch-only household assistant** (idea 1) — proven buildable by one person (Stevens); differentiation from free vendor assistants is the risk.
  6. **Spec-first verification tooling** (idea 12) and **niche data migration** (idea 11) — technically ripe, demand unproven.
- Avoid as head-on projects: meeting notes and e-mail triage (10), general tutor (7), general health-record app (6), general agent (1), vibe-coding platform (4), COBOL modernisation (5a), total-recall screen recorder (3).
- A design rule that applies to every survivor: keep a checker outside the model (rules engine, validator, tests, proof checker, or a person approving a draft) and never let the product both read untrusted content and send data out on its own.

### Gaps
- All filter verdicts rest on CLAIMED evidence; F1 for ideas 2, 3, 11 and 12 is the least supported and should be cross-checked against the demand evidence gathered by the other researchers.
- I could not check whether narrow products already exist for the suggested survivors (e.g. an agentic screen reader shipped by a screen-reader vendor or OS maker, or a consumer cross-border form tool); "least crowded" means "nothing surfaced in search summaries".
- Regulatory exposure for idea 8 (unauthorised practice of law, tax-preparer rules) and idea 6 (health-data law) differs by country and was not researched beyond the US items cited.
