# Software wishes that recur across Hacker News wish threads, 2014–2026 (read at the source, 27 Nov 2026)

> **How far to trust these notes.**
> - **VERIFIED** = I fetched the thread or comment through the Hacker News API (hn.algolia.com `items/<id>`) in this session and read it. Every HN quote below is VERIFIED and was machine-checked against the downloaded text after writing.
> - **VERIFIED-META** = I fetched an HN story's title, date, points and comment count from the API but did not read the linked page.
> - **CLAIMED** = from a web-search summary or a store/vendor listing; not read at the source.
> - What I read: 77 wish-type threads (2014–2026) with 1,643 top-level comments. For 72 of them (1,145 top-level comments) I read the first ~400 characters of every top-level comment. For four developer-tool threads (212 comments) I read only the most-replied comments plus keyword counts. For the 286-comment "software you made only for yourself" thread I read the 40 most-replied comments plus keyword counts. I then read the full reply trees under about 95 wish comments.
> - HN does not publish votes on comments. Where I give `r=N`, N is the number of replies under the comment, the only engagement figure available.
> - Counts per wish are from my own hand classification of the comments I read. They are small numbers. A wish with 16 comments over 12 years is what "recurring" means in this material.
> - Reddit was not consulted (blocked). github.com pages were read through the fetch tool, which returns a model-written summary of the page; star counts and README wording from GitHub are therefore VERIFIED as fetched but the wording was not machine-checked. The GitHub API was not available.

## Which wish threads exist on Hacker News, 2014–2026?

### Takeaway
The question is asked on HN many times a year (I found 77 threads with wish-type titles and at least a handful of answers), but the large ones are all from 2016–2022; since 2023 a typical wish thread gets 5–50 comments. The two biggest recent threads that carry wish evidence are not phrased as wish threads at all: "software you made only for your own use" (July 2024, 246 points, 463 comments) and "your next idea should focus on aged care" (July 2024, 130 points, 137 comments).

### Inferences
- The wish-thread format lost its audience after 2022. A ranked shortlist cannot lean on 2024–2026 wish threads alone: the twelve general ones found (leaving out the developer-tool, API, privacy-tool, small-business, own-use and aged-care threads) hold 116 top-level comments in total.
- For recent evidence the better HN sources are topic threads ("how do you manage X?") and the own-use thread, which shows what people cared about enough to build.

### Gaps
- Algolia's search matches loosely and caps results; threads with unusual titles may have been missed. I ran more than 100 distinct search phrases across four passes; coverage of threads with 40+ comments is probably near complete, coverage of small ones is not.
- Threads before 2014 were not read.

## Which specific wishes recur, how often, and which are still being asked in 2024–2026?

### Takeaway
Eleven software wishes recur across at least four independent threads; the most frequent are "take the life admin off me" (21 comments, 16 threads, 20 people, 2014–2026), a filtered news or social feed (16 comments, 10 threads), finding anything I have seen or own (16 comments, 12 threads), help with health questions and records (17 comments, 13 threads) and tax or bookkeeping (11 comments, 11 threads). The single most persistent wish of all is a machine that folds laundry (20 comments, 11 threads, 2016–2026), which is hardware.

### Inferences
- Counting distinct people matters. The Memex wish looks alive in 2023–2025 only because one user repeats it; hearing aids likewise. W1, W6, W7 and W10 are the wishes with many different askers in both the old and the recent threads.
- W8 and W9, which the earlier unverified notes treated as leading candidates, are weakly supported in the wish threads after 2022.
- W5 barely appears in threads titled as wish threads, yet draws 137–370 comments when asked directly. Wish threads under-sample problems that programmers have on behalf of someone else.

### Gaps
- No vote counts on individual comments exist in the API, so "how many people agreed" cannot be measured beyond reply counts.
- Classification was by one reader in one pass. Borderline comments (for example a general "Jarvis" request) were left out; a different reader could move counts by two or three per wish.
- A search of all HN comments from 2025–2026 for ten wish phrases ("I wish there was", "I would pay for", "why is there no" and others) returned 2,349 comments — [Algolia query](https://hn.algolia.com/api/v1/search_by_date?query=%22I%20wish%20there%20was%22&tags=comment&numericFilters=created_at_i%3E1735689600). Keyword tagging put about 9% on developer tooling and 6% on hardware; the buckets for documents, health, tax, elder care and personal finance were each under 1%, and on reading them almost all were false matches (the word appears, the wish is about something else). Phrase search is not a usable demand signal for these wishes.

## What obstacle blocked each wish, and does a very capable LLM remove it?

### Takeaway
People who tried report four kinds of obstacle: messy input and long-tail per-source work (which a capable model removes), access to other parties' accounts and platforms (which it does not), liability for the result (which it does not), and customers who will not pay (which it does not). W1, W2 and W5 are mostly held back by the first kind plus trust; W7 and W6 by liability and regulation; W8 by incentives; W3, W9 and W11 by weak willingness to pay.

### Cited Findings

Obstacles stated by people who tried or who work in the field (all VERIFIED)
- Access and trust (W1, W3): "even if it did, just read access to email would grant access to all my online accounts via password reset or magic link emails." — [HN 14678351](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=14678351) (2017)
- Platform owners close the door (W3, W10): "Readwise was close to this until Twitter shut off API access." — [HN 40515006](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=40515006) (2024). A 2026 commenter expects the same for an independent YouTube search: "likely google would block the services from its api." — [HN 46966848](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46966848)
- Security design works against helping someone remotely (W5): "The problem here is that I'm basically asking for something equivalent to a one click exploit." — [HN 32943180](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=32943180) (2022)
- Liability (W7 and legal documents): "Computers can't yet assume such liability." — [HN 13342895](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=13342895) (2017); "to the provider they kind of have to provide a very robust calculator and probably assume some liability." — [HN 17399251](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=17399251) (2018)

- Wishes that were blocked by understanding unstructured input stopped being asked once models could do it: podcast transcription ([HN 15602967](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=15602967), 2017), PDF data extraction ([HN 15112975](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=15112975), 2017), resume rewriting ([HN 37205850](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=37205850), 2023).

- ChatGPT's agent was reported clicking through an "I am not a robot" check (VERIFIED-META, 2025-07-28, 255 points, 292 comments) — [HN 44716653](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44716653), which bears on whether an agent can operate third-party sites for W1 and W5.

### Inferences
Per wish: the obstacle, and whether a very capable model removes it.
- W1 life admin. Obstacle before: each provider has its own portal, phone tree and letter format, so only a human assistant could do it, at $100–400 a month. A model removes the understanding and the long tail of portals and calls. It does not remove account access (two-factor codes, bot blocks), the risk of a wrong irreversible action, or the trust a person must place in it. Partly removed.
- W2 documents. Obstacle before: rule-based classification failed on varied documents; self-hosting excluded most people. The first is removed. The second is a matter of running a service and being trusted with sensitive papers. Removed as a capability; open as a product for people who do not self-host.
- W5 elderly relatives. Obstacle before: the hard part is hostile third-party websites and login flows, which no simplified device fixes. A model that can operate any website on the person's behalf, with a relative approving, attacks exactly that. It does not remove banks' anti-automation rules, fraud exposure, or the question of who pays. Plausibly removed technically; commercial obstacle remains.
- W4 photos. Obstacle before: near-duplicate judgment and triage did not scale without manual review. Vision models remove that. Immich already covers self-hosters. Mostly removed; what is left is triage on the phone's own library.
- W10 feed filter. Obstacle before: judging what is noise needed a person, and platforms closed their APIs. A model supplies the judgment and can run in the browser on the page as displayed, which needs no API. The digest half is served by existing publications. Removed for the filter variant, with a per-item cost question.
- W11 page monitoring. Obstacle was deciding which changes matter. Removed, and already shipped.
- W7 tax and books. Obstacle: liability, rules that differ by country and change yearly, and in the US a filing channel controlled by the state and incumbents. A model helps with the rules' long tail and with reading documents. It does not carry liability. Not removed for filing; partly removed for preparation and checking.
- W6 health. Obstacle: regulation, liability, and access to records. General chat assistants have absorbed the "help me think about my symptoms" half. Not a one-engineer opening.
- W8 choice. Obstacle: whoever ranks products is paid by sellers. A model does the reading, but the 2026 advertising products inside ChatGPT show the incentive problem moving with it. Tradespeople: not a software problem per those who tried. Not removed.
- W3 personal search. Obstacles: access to every account, privacy fear, and few concrete uses. Embeddings and local models improve retrieval; they do not create demand. Not the binding constraint.
- W9 local events. The wish threads contain no first-hand account of the obstacle; the earlier notes (unverified) put it at per-venue scraping, which a model removes, and at monetisation, which it does not.

### Gaps
- No thread compares an attempt made before capable models with one made after; the inferences above connect separate statements.
- Whether banks, utilities and government portals in practice tolerate an agent acting for a customer was not researched.

### Inferences
- The non-programmer wishes on HN are those of well-paid people short of time, and of adult children of ageing parents. Wishes of people short of money appear once (the paycheck budget) in the material read. A shortlist built from HN will tilt toward saving time for the comfortable.
- The most consistent non-programmer theme, across paperwork, money and elder care, is the same job: read an official letter, bill or web form, work out what it wants, and get it done or filed. That one job underlies W1, W2, W5 and the preparation half of W7.

### Gaps
- No non-HN forum was read, so there is no check on how far HN's mix departs from the general public's. Reddit remains unreachable from this session.
- I did not classify all 1,145 comments into programmer and non-programmer wishes; the proportions above come from keyword counts on two samples, not a full hand count.
- German-language or other non-English demand (for example club administration or German tax tools) is represented by single comments only ([HN 12571449](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=12571449), [HN 46032769](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46032769)).
