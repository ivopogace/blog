# Forum cross-check (Hacker News and Stack Exchange) of ten candidate software ideas, as of 27 Nov 2026

> **Read this first: how far to trust these notes.**
> - **VERIFIED** means I fetched the item through the Hacker News Algolia API (`hn.algolia.com/api/v1/...`) or the Stack Exchange API and read the returned text myself. Links point to the public page of the same item. Quotes are copied from the API text with HTML removed; "…" marks where I cut a quote. **CLAIMED** means second-hand. Unless a bullet says otherwise, everything below is VERIFIED.
> - "p" = story points, "c" = comment count, both as returned by the HN search API on 27 Nov 2026. Hacker News does not publish votes on individual comments, so comment quotes carry no vote count.
> - "Title only" means I saw the title, date and counts but did not read the comments.
> - Limits of the method: (1) HN search requires all query words to match, so comment-level searches with several words often return nothing; "no hits" means "not found with the queries I ran", not proof of absence. (2) Hacker News readers are mostly programmers, mostly in the US. It is a poor place to look for benefit claimants, older people, small-practice doctors, German welfare law or UK disability benefits. Where a verdict says "thin on these forums", read it with that in mind. (3) The Software Recommendations site is small (23,394 questions) and dominated by 2014–2016 questions about developer and desktop utilities.
> - GitHub figures and README quotes were read through the fetch tool, which returns an extract of the page; treat README wording as the tool's extraction of the page text.
> - Hosts refused in this session: `harryherzberg.substack.com` and `blog.cloudflare.com` (not resolvable), `github.com` through curl and the `gh` API (403 / not enabled). GitHub pages were readable through the fetch tool. Reddit was not attempted. No web searches were used; the shared search budget is untouched.
> - About 37 Stack Exchange API calls were made (softwarerecs, academia, money, expatriates).
> - Corrections to earlier notes found on the way are collected at the end of section 1.

## 1. For each of the ten candidates: what independent forum evidence of demand exists, and is it spread over years and people or one loud thread?

### Takeaway
Only three candidates have broad, multi-year, multi-person demand on these forums: contesting US health-insurance denials and medical bills (candidate 1), "all local events in one place" (candidate 8), and a self-run personal or family assistant (second half of candidate 9). For the other candidates the forums show small product launches with almost no response and no threads in which people ask for the product; for the non-US and non-technical candidates this is partly a limit of the forums.

## 3. For each: what do builders who tried say blocked them, and which blockers does a very capable model remove?

### Takeaway
Builders and experienced commenters name five blockers again and again: nobody checks whether the model's reading of the rules is right; the target user does not trust an agent with money or identity; data lives in closed places (Instagram, practice systems, phones used as second factor); small organisations and people in a crisis will not adopt new software; and no one pays. A stronger model helps directly with only the first and with scraper upkeep.

### Inferences
- What a very capable model removes: reading the denial, bill, letter or tender; drafting; keeping scrapers alive when sites change (candidate 8); writing small apps (9a). ### Inferences
- Where the surveys and the forums disagree (candidates 2, 3, 6, 10, and candidate 1 outside the US), the likeliest explanation is who reads Hacker News, not that the surveys are wrong. The forum evidence still adds something the surveys cannot: for each of these, several people have already shipped a small tool and got no visible uptake. That is a warning about reaching users, not about the need.
- Where they agree (candidate 1 in the US, candidate 8), the niche is also the most crowded. The cross-check found no candidate that is both clearly wanted on these forums and clearly empty.
- The two places where the forum evidence should change the ranking most: candidate 9a moves down (Cloudflare OS), and candidate 4 should be reframed from "agent that acts" to "helper that reads, explains and prepares, with the user confirming", because the intended users say they will not delegate money or applications.
- Candidate 7's forum evidence supports the earlier note's doubt: the pain is described at length, the paid tool is rejected for reasons a better model does not touch (price, trust, longevity, the family conversation).
- No candidate here needs hardware or regulatory approval. Data nobody can get: Instagram-only and Facebook-only events (candidate 8) and practice-system records (candidate 10). None is mainly a surveillance, manipulation or spam tool; candidates 1, 2 and 6 carry the "flooding" side effect when they submit in bulk, which commenters and the receiving bodies already complain about ([49422227](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49422227), [44674959](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44674959)).

### Gaps
- These verdicts rest on two forum families only. A verdict of "thinner" for candidates 2, 3, 4, 6, 7 and 10 should be re-tested where their users actually write (Reddit, Mumsnet, AgingCare, Benefits and Work, German forums, physician and nonprofit communities) before any of them is dropped.
- Willingness to pay was not measurable for any candidate; the only direct price reaction found is the objection to LastShelf's roughly $100 a year.
- The 2026 state of the named products beyond what HN threads and GitHub pages show was not checked.
