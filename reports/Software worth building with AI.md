# A decade of wishes converges on paperwork

The project most worth building is a **supervised web-errand helper for an older relative**: software that sits in an older person's browser, reads whatever bill-payment page, login or form is in front of them, explains in plain words what it wants and walks them through it, and passes any step that touches money or identity to a named family member who approves or takes over. It leads because it is the only candidate in the research where three things hold together. The demand was read at the source and is recent and broad: Hacker News threads of 370, 230 and 137 comments between 2021 and 2026, with 2026 accounts of a relative "driven to tears" by ordinary websites ([HN 40127400](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=40127400), [HN 47042747](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47042747)). No product that does this job was found, only simplified devices and screen sharing. And a strong model removes the exact obstacle, because the trouble is other people's websites, which no locked-down tablet can fix and which only a patient human could read until now. It is a narrow lead, not a clean pass: the best agent completed 20.6% of hour-long computer workflows in the June 2026 OSWorld 2.0 paper ([arXiv 2606.29537](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29537)), bank logins resist any helper, two founders with experience in elder technology say families do not pay, and the people asking are adult children, not the older users themselves. **No idea in the research passes all five filters without qualification**, so this report ranks six candidates and names the filter each one only partly passes. The pattern behind the ranking is that every wish clearly confirmed on the forums already sits in a crowded niche, and that the one job recurring across twelve years of wish threads is the same everywhere: read an official letter, bill or web form, work out what it wants, and get it done.

## Reddit, vendor boards and most European sources were never reached

The ranking rests on three bodies of evidence read at the source on 27 November 2026: 77 Hacker News wish threads from 2014 to 2026 with 1,643 top-level comments, the largest of them a 2016 thread with 526 comments ([HN 12567645](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=12567645)); a cross-check of ten candidate ideas against Hacker News and Stack Exchange; and a sweep of about 150 GitHub and GitLab issue trackers sorted by thumbs-up, with 24 threads read. A verification pass then checked 67 statistics from the earlier, snippet-based notes at their primary pages: 46 held, 8 needed correction, 6 were not on the page cited and 7 could not be opened. Where that pass corrected a figure, this report uses the corrected one.

**Reddit was unreachable throughout.** **Vendor feedback boards were blocked** (Atlassian's Jira, Microsoft's Feedback Portal, Mozilla and LibreOffice Bugzilla, and the Obsidian, Zotero, Spotify and Figma forums), so the feature-request evidence covers developer tools on GitHub and says nothing about office or consumer software. **Most UK and German primary sources stayed closed** (gov.uk, arbeitsagentur.de, policyinpractice.co.uk, vdk.de, kfw.de, kbv.de), as did ftc.gov, oecd.org, metr.org and snorkel.ai, so almost every UK and German statistic below is claimed, not verified. Carer and claimant forums (Mumsnet, AgingCare, Benefits and Work) were seen only through search summaries.

Hacker News readers are mostly programmers and mostly American. A verdict of "thinner on the forums" for a German, British or non-technical idea means untested there, not disproved. Hacker News publishes no votes on comments, so the counts below are small hand-classified numbers of comments and distinct people; a wish with 16 comments over 12 years is what "recurring" means in this material. No product was tested and no usage or revenue figure was found for any product named. Commenters in the wish threads warn that stated willingness to pay predicts little: "most people will not actually pay for something they say they will pay for when asked for an off-the-cuff idea" ([HN 16014681](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=16014681)).

How claims are marked: a cited fact with no tag was read at its source by the researchers. **[C]** marks a claim seen only in a search-result summary, a vendor listing or a press piece that was not opened. **[title only]** marks a Hacker News thread whose title, date and counts were fetched but whose comments were not read, and **[listing only]** a GitHub issue seen in the API listing but not opened. GitHub pages were read through a fetch tool that returns an extract, so README wording is as that tool reported it. Product names that post-date the researchers' knowledge appear as the sources gave them; Cloudflare OS, OpenClaw and ChatGPT Health were each confirmed through a source fetched directly (the HN API, GitHub or Wikipedia), and model names that could not be confirmed on any opened page are left out.

## Wherever demand was confirmed, the niche was already crowded

Eleven software wishes recur across at least four independent wish threads. Counted by distinct people, the leaders are "take the life admin off me" (**21 comments from 20 people in 16 threads, 2014 to 2026**), help with health questions and records (17 comments, 17 people), a filtered news or social feed (16 comments, 15 people), finding anything I have seen or own (16 comments, though three of the recent ones come from one user) and tax or bookkeeping (11 comments, 11 people). The single most persistent wish, a machine that folds laundry (20 comments in 11 threads, still asked in 2026), is hardware ([HN 48046467](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48046467)). Repetition alone does not point to buildable software.

The threads also show what a capable model changes. Wishes blocked by unstructured input stopped being asked once models could handle it: podcast transcripts ([HN 15602967](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=15602967)), PDF data extraction ([HN 15112975](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=15112975)), resume rewriting ([HN 37205850](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=37205850)). People who tried to build the remaining wishes report four kinds of obstacle: messy input and long-tail per-source work, access to other parties' accounts and platforms, liability for the result, and customers who will not pay. A model removes the first and none of the other three. The GitHub sweep shows the developer-side version of the same thing. Maintainers rarely refuse a request; they say they have no capacity. In 2026 a burst of one-person tools answered requests that had sat open for years: four comment exporters for Overleaf ([overleaf#1126](https://github.com/overleaf/overleaf/issues/1126)), five Git tools for FreeCAD files ([FreeCAD#11936](https://github.com/FreeCAD/FreeCAD/issues/11936)), three BPMN implementations for Mermaid ([mermaid#2623](https://github.com/mermaid-js/mermaid/issues/2623)). The window is open but not empty.

The forum cross-check tested ten candidates drawn from the survey-based notes. It confirmed demand for only three (contesting US health-insurance denials, all local events in one place, and a self-run family assistant among technical users) and found each of them crowded. For benefits navigators, letter explainers, grant tools, estate tools and clinic paperwork it found one pattern: a string of launches with one to eight points and no thread in which anyone asks. That shows oversupply of small tools more clearly than absence of need, because the people with those problems do not read Hacker News. **The cross-check found no candidate that is both clearly wanted on these forums and clearly empty**, which is why the table below has no row of five passes.

Three moves in this ranking come straight from the cross-check. Contesting a wrong decision was the best-evidenced burden in the survey notes and ranks third because its US niche is crowded. Safe hosting for self-made apps dropped out when Cloudflare OS launched in August 2026. The accessibility agent, ranked first among the "old dreams", is folded into candidate 1 in a narrower form, because blind users on the forums say they will not let software move money for them.

## Six candidates, ranked, each with the filter it only partly passes

The first-version sketches are this report's inference from the obstacles the sources describe; no source proposes them in this form.

### 1. A supervised web-errand helper for an older relative

**What it is.** A helper in the older person's browser that reads the page in front of them, says in plain words what it wants, and guides the next step. Any step that moves money or proves identity goes to a named relative, who sees the same page remotely and approves or takes over.

**Evidence of demand.** "Ask HN: How to manage phones and PCs for elderly parents?" drew **278 points and 370 comments** in April 2024 ([HN 40127400](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=40127400)); one answer sums up the support calls: "100% of the time it involves logging in to a website or paying for something" ([HN 40127907](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=40127907)). "Tell HN: your next idea should focus on aged care" drew 137 comments three months later ([HN 40951324](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=40951324)); its most-replied answer says "We basically need an entire tier of easy internet for the elderly" ([HN 40951431](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=40951431)), and a reply names "trusted verified handling of regular financial life interactions, bill paying" ([HN 40951697](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=40951697)). A February 2026 thread reports a bill payment that took 24 minutes to find and then timed out during an emailed code ([HN 47042756](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47042756)) and a bank login that "Fails 100% of the time" ([HN 47042855](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47042855)). Earlier comments say "I'd gladly pay someone to do it right, but I just can't find anyone" ([HN 34428363](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=34428363)) and "I would pay good money" for one-click screen sharing ([HN 32941909](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=32941909)). Three more threads of 71 to 230 comments cover the same ground [title only] ([HN 29429558](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=29429558), [HN 35410162](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=35410162), [HN 29685115](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=29685115)). Outside the forums, AARP counts 63 million US family caregivers giving 27 hours a week on average, who want help "managing complex paperwork" ([AARP caregiving report](https://www.aarp.org/content/dam/aarp/ppi/topics/ltss/family-caregiving/caregiving-in-us-2025.doi.10.26419-2fppi.00373.001.pdf)), and reports AI use among Americans aged 50 and over rising from 18% to 30% in a year ([AARP tech trends](https://www.aarp.org/pri/topics/technology/internet-media-devices/2026-technology-trends-older-adults/)). **Where demand is thinner than it looks:** the wish appears in only four comments across three threads titled as wish threads, the voices are programmers speaking for a parent, and the 2024 thread's most-replied advice is to buy a locked-down mainstream device, not new software.

**Closest attempts and why they fall short.** The 2024 thread recommends moving parents to an iPhone ([HN 40129234](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=40129234)), a Mac ([HN 40127599](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=40127599)) or ChromeOS ([HN 40129162](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=40129162)) and using built-in screen sharing. Commenters also name True Link Financial for supervised spending ([HN 40951722](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=40951722)) and Ordissimo, Eldy, Endless OS and iPhone Assistive Access ([HN 40951653](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=40951653)). Search summaries add simplified tablets such as GrandPad [C] ([DailyCaring](https://dailycaring.com/grandpad-easy-to-use-tech-solution-helps-families-connect-with-seniors-and-prevent-social-isolation)), Carefull's account monitoring and bill help [C] ([citybiz](https://www.citybiz.co/article/477940/carefull-n-y-fintech-to-protect-seniors-closes-on-16-5-million-series-a/)) and Jortty's fraud-focused "tech concierge" [C] ([FinTech Futures](https://www.fintechfutures.com/?p=15252328)). All of these simplify the device or watch the accounts, while the problem commenters describe is other people's websites. General agents such as ChatGPT agent and Cowork [title only] ([HN 44595492](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44595492), [HN 46593022](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46593022)) are built for a capable user who supervises them. No source described a product that completes arbitrary web tasks for an older person under a relative's supervision; that absence comes from search summaries and is not proof.

**The obstacle AI removes.** Every bank, utility and government site has its own layout, wording and login ritual, so the only thing that could cope was a patient human. A model that reads any page removes that per-site long tail. A diary study of 27 older adults found their technology questions verbose, incomplete or mis-specified, and that having a model rephrase them raised automated solution accuracy from 35% to 69% ([arXiv 2601.10018](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.10018)).

**Hardest remaining problem.** Reliability and trust on the steps that matter. The OSWorld 2.0 paper reports the best agent completing **20.6% of hour-long computer workflows** strictly and 54.8% with partial credit ([arXiv 2606.29537](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29537)); later leaderboard entries in the mid-40s were seen claimed and could not be checked [C] ([Snorkel leaderboard](https://snorkel.ai/leaderboard/os-world-2-0/)). On 300 tasks across 136 live websites the two best 2025 agents reached 56% and 61% ([arXiv 2504.01382](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01382)). In a three-week study an agent completed 52.5% of 1,258 commands from eight blind users ([arXiv 2609.00524](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00524)). Blind commenters refuse to delegate banking for exactly this reason: "I don't want to miss a bill payment because AI thought it knew what I wanted and sent money to the wrong place" ([HN 42743016](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=42743016)). One commenter observes that helping someone remotely amounts to "something equivalent to a one click exploit" ([HN 32943180](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=32943180)). Whether banks and government portals tolerate an assisted session was not researched. The market itself is disputed by people who tried: "It’s basically a no go market. Patients don’t have the funds themselves" ([HN 40955937](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=40955937)); "There is no money in this business to make" ([HN 40951712](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=40951712)).

**First version.** A browser extension for one parent and one relative. It explains the current page in large type or speech, highlights the next click, and has one button that sends the relative a summary and a live view. It stores no passwords and never pays by itself. It starts with the errands the threads name: paying a utility bill, getting through a login, cancelling or renewing a subscription.

### 2. A hosted case file for household paperwork

**What it is.** A phone-first service where a household photographs or forwards every letter, bill and notice. It extracts sender, amount, deadline and what is being asked, keeps each matter together over months, and prepares the reply or form for the person to sign and send.

**Evidence of demand.** "Take the life admin off me" is the most frequent software wish in the 77 threads, with four of its 21 comments from 2024 and two from 2026. The most-replied answer in one 2016 thread asks for "a private secretary who takes care of all the small things like booking flights, booking hotels, doing taxes, sorting mail, sorting documents" ([HN 13115361](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=13115361)); a 2021 answer about problems worth more than $1,000 is "Never having to do paperwork ever again" ([HN 29025741](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=29025741)). The document-filing half has six wish comments plus dedicated threads, the largest with **261 points and 179 comments** ([HN 29161110](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=29161110)): "I'd pay good money for a software/service that manages my documents, from scanning to archiving" ([HN 34427596](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=34427596)); on receipts, "No app does this right" ([HN 38987628](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=38987628)). This is the only wish with in-thread evidence of money changing hands: one commenter paid an agency three to four times $100 a month ([HN 14678255](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=14678255)), another employed a remote assistant and called it "well worth it" ([HN 13115615](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=13115615)). **Where demand is thinner than it looks:** the cross-check found no thread in which anyone asks for a letter explainer, a 2026 launch of one drew 3 points and no comments ([HN 48638999](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48638999)), and a form-filling launch was met with "how is this different from me uploading to chatgpt and asking it to fill in?" ([HN 47985570](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47985570)). A German survey claim that three in four people do not understand official German and that 41% have paid a late fee as a result is unverified [C] ([t-online](https://www.t-online.de/nachrichten/tagesanbruch/id_100424760/rente-steuern-erbe-deutsche-verstehen-amtliche-schreiben-nicht.html)) and untested on the forums.

**Closest attempts and why they fall short.** paperless-ngx has 48.7k stars, but its README offers no hosted service or mobile app and says the safest way to run it is "on a local server in your own home" ([GitHub: paperless-ngx](https://github.com/paperless-ngx/paperless-ngx)); the add-ons paperless-ai (6.2k stars) and paperless-gpt (2.9k) already use models to fill in titles, tags, correspondents and dates ([GitHub: paperless-ai](https://github.com/clusterzx/paperless-ai), [GitHub: paperless-gpt](https://github.com/icereed/paperless-gpt)). The asker of the 2023 wish wanted the opposite of self-hosting: "a hosted service that I can use from my desktop or phone to upload documents quickly" ([HN 34430469](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=34430469)). At least six phone apps launched in 2025 and 2026 that photograph letters and extract senders, amounts and deadlines, known only from vendor listings [C] ([Google Play: Docriva](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.docriva.app)); Totoy in Vienna explains letters in 18 languages [C] ([Der Standard](https://www.derstandard.at/story/3000000187573/app-wien-uebersetzung-behoerden-deutsch)). General chatbots explain any single letter. Nothing found holds a matter across months and produces the reply.

**The obstacle AI removes.** Rule-based classification failed on varied documents, because every sender formats letters differently. That obstacle is gone, and the open-source add-ons prove it. This is why the candidate only partly passes filter 3: what remains is hosting and trust, which a model does not supply.

**Hardest remaining problem.** Being trusted with a household's most sensitive papers. A 2026 launch of a family document map was met with "why yes, i would love to give a vibecoded app access to all of my important information" ([HN 48849439](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48849439)), "a hundred dollars a year for this??" ([HN 48848151](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48848151)) and doubt that the site would outlive a bank's safe-deposit box ([HN 48849886](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48849886)). Email intake raises the oldest objection in the threads: "just read access to email would grant access to all my online accounts via password reset or magic link emails" ([HN 14678351](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=14678351)). The price ceiling is low: "$5 problems are often not worth paying to solve" ([HN 13301625](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=13301625)).

**First version.** Photo and forwarding intake for one country's letters; a card per document showing each extracted field beside the passage it came from; a calendar of deadlines; a drafted reply or pre-filled form as a PDF. No account access and no sending. It shares its reading engine with candidate 1 and can be built as the same product.

### 3. Appeals against wrong decisions, with the checking built in

**What it is.** A tool that turns a denial letter into an appeal and checks its own work: that each cited rule exists and applies to this plan, that the amounts recompute, and that the packet goes to the right body before the deadline. The person signs and sends; the tool never files in bulk.

**Evidence of demand.** Six Hacker News threads between 2023 and 2026, from at least five submitters, deal with patients fighting insurers and hospitals. The largest, "Using AI to negotiate a $195k hospital bill down to $33k", reached **1,034 points and 912 comments** ([HN 45734582](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45734582)); "Using AI to fight insurance claim denials" reached 203 points ([HN 41358132](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=41358132)). Commenters ask in their own words: "i'd love to fight my insurance, but it's so unnecessarily confusing" ([HN 41359127](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=41359127)); "it's an asymmetric game" ([HN 41358739](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=41358739)). Statistics verified at the source agree. On HealthCare.gov plans in 2024 about 85 million in-network claims were denied, **fewer than 1% were appealed**, and insurers upheld 66% of those appeals ([KFF, March 2026](https://www.kff.org/patient-consumer-protections/claims-denials-and-appeals-in-aca-marketplace-plans-in-2024/)). Of people with a denied claim, 69% did not know whether they had appeal rights ([KFF survey, 2023](https://www.kff.org/affordable-care-act/consumer-survey-highlights-problems-with-denied-health-insurance-claims/)). **43% of appealed prior-authorisation denials were overturned** in the federal Marketplace in 2025 ([KFF, August 2026](https://www.kff.org/patient-consumer-protections/prior-authorization-metrics-provide-new-insights-into-insurer-practices-but-gaps-remain/)). Physicians complete 40 prior authorisations a week and only 21% always appeal an adverse decision ([AMA survey](https://www.ama-assn.org/press-center/ama-press-releases/ama-survey-prior-authorization-reform-pledge-falls-short-physicians)). **Where demand is thinner than it looks:** KFF says its survey "cannot tell how often claims denials are incorrect"; a commenter points out the selection bias in overturn rates ([HN 49702264](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49702264)); and the largest thread is a story about a general chatbot, which shows the demand and shows that the chatbot already meets much of it. For the UK and Germany the figures are claimed only: 67% of disability-benefit tribunal appeals won [C] ([Benefits and Work](https://benefitsandwork.co.uk/news/tribunal-backlog-soars)), and 476,728 objections against basic-income notices decided in 2025 with 30.9% upheld [C] ([buergergeld.org](https://www.buergergeld.org/news/fehlerhafte-buergergeld-bescheide-jeder-dritte-widerspruch-ist-erfolgreich/)); that benefit has been called Grundsicherungsgeld since 1 July 2026 ([German Wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%BCrgergeld)). Forum searches for both returned nothing, which reflects who reads Hacker News.

**Closest attempts and why they fall short.** In the US the niche is crowded. Fight Health Insurance is free and open source (156 stars, 1,844 commits) and drafts appeals ([GitHub: fighthealthinsurance](https://github.com/totallylegitco/fighthealthinsurance)); its own launch got 3 points ([HN 41356832](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=41356832)) and its intake was called "Probably overwhelming to a non-dev" ([HN 41358789](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=41358789)). Claimable charges about $50 a case and files by fax and post for a fixed list of conditions [C] ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-04-22/ai-and-mark-cuban-among-startup-s-tools-to-fight-denied-health-care-claims)); a would-be user found neither their condition nor their treatment on its lists ([HN 47866563](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47866563)). Counterforce Health drafts letters free and does not collect outcomes [C] ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/local/raleigh/2025/08/20/using-ai-to-fight-back-against-insurance-denials-counteforce)). General chatbots are the tool in the largest thread, and a New York Times review found they "sometimes gave wrong advice, misunderstanding laws or payer intricacies" [C] ([The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2026/04/09/patients-are-using-chatbots-to-fight-medical-bills-with-mixed-results)). No dedicated product was found for UK or German benefit appeals, though the search for one was cut short.

**The obstacle AI removes.** Each case is a unique pile of unstructured documents under local rules, and reading and drafting are now cheap. What nobody checks is whether the model's reading of the rules is right: "it doesn't seem like the accuracy of its understanding of medicare practices was actually checked" drew 92 replies ([HN 45734873](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45734873)). A vendor-authored preprint found frontier models perfect on simple eligibility routes and failing on nested exception chains, with a deterministic engine beating end-to-end model evaluation ([arXiv 2607.23386](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23386)). Builders in the field already work this way: "we only perform data entry in cases where we can deterministically verify that the information was correctly entered" ([HN 44238124](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44238124)).

**Hardest remaining problem.** Building and maintaining the check outside the model, one payer type or jurisdiction at a time, without claiming to be a lawyer. DoNotPay paid $193,000 to settle FTC charges over its "AI lawyer" ([HN 41659324](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=41659324)); the order itself could not be opened, and a commenter quoting the FTC notes the company retained no lawyers to verify outputs ([HN 41659665](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=41659665)). The receiving side is adapting. A study of "agentic flooding" collected 84 potential cases across 11 jurisdictions, including German social-court filings where "some letters spanned over 4000 pages", and warns that added friction "disproportionately deters poorer, less digitally literate" users ([arXiv 2608.16603](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16603)). German social courts are reported to have warned about AI filings that cite invented rulings [C] ([gegen-hartz.de](https://www.gegen-hartz.de/news/sozialgerichte-warnen-vor-ki-klagen-erfundene-urteile)), and German law reserves individual legal advice for licensed providers [C] ([beck-aktuell](https://www.beck-aktuell.de/rechtsbranche/legal-tech-digitalisierung/chatgpt-individuelle-rechtsberatung-ki-jura-rechtsdienstleistung-2025-11-11)).

**First version.** US prior-authorisation denials only, the category with the best verified overturn figure. It asks the plan type first, produces an appeal in which every cited provision is quoted and linked, shows the deadline and the right regulator, and says plainly when a case needs a human. One packet per person, signed and sent by them.

### 4. One city's events, with scrapers an agent keeps alive

**What it is.** A single web page, RSS feed and calendar file listing what is on in one city, collected from venue sites, ticket platforms and community calendars. A coding agent writes and repairs the per-source scrapers on a schedule.

**Evidence of demand.** Threads by different people run from 2009 to 2026: "Meetup.com alternatives" (2019, **722 points, 286 comments**) ([HN 21257661](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=21257661)), a Meetup alternative's launch (2024, 351 points) ([HN 40717398](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=40717398)) and a "Things to Do API" (October 2026, 57 points) ([HN 50126384](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50126384)). In them: "I'd love to have a single place that could let me search and link me out to events listed on Facebook, Eventbrite" ([HN 40730936](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=40730936)); "I always wanted something similar for my city" ([HN 39461344](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=39461344)); "This feels hard to maintain and keep up-to-date, but I would definitely use it!" ([HN 50127176](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50127176)). The cross-check judged this wish general, with little forum skew. **Where demand is thinner than it looks:** in the wish threads proper it appears five times in twelve years and not since 2022; "I would pay $10/month for new things to do in my city" is from 2016 ([HN 12401031](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=12401031)). The two biggest threads are about platforms for organisers, not aggregators for the public.

**Closest attempts and why they fall short.** Nine launches between 2024 and 2026, most under 20 points ([HN 39459456](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=39459456), [HN 44029084](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44029084), [HN 46229810](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46229810)). The users' complaint is coverage: "6 or 7 events listed for the entire Burlington, Vermont metro area (over 200,000 people)" ([HN 39460923](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=39460923)). The one project found that used scheduled coding-agent routines to maintain its scrapers, Seattle's 206events, had 5 stars and was archived by its owner on 19 October 2026 ([GitHub: 206events](https://github.com/prestomation/206events)); the notes do not record why. A founder's write-up counting a "Graveyard of ~200 companies" could not be opened [C] ([Medium](https://medium.com/@viking2917/so-you-re-thinking-about-a-things-to-do-app-f31d2a608580)).

**The obstacle AI removes.** "getting users is easy, getting events is the hard part!" wrote a builder in 2019 ([HN 21264225](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=21264225)). Someone who ran a city listing for about ten years found it "difficult to maintain, especially since sources (APIs / websites) tend to change frequently" ([HN 39461344](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=39461344)). A 2024 builder saw no way "other than writing custom beautiful soup or similar code for every single source" ([HN 39460373](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=39460373)). A model reads arbitrary pages and repairs extractors when they break; a 2026 builder answered the maintenance worry with "If an agent maintains it, is it hard? :D" ([HN 50134879](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50134879)).

**Hardest remaining problem.** Events that exist only inside Instagram or Facebook, which is data nobody outside can get: "It's a real problem if you don't want to spend time on that app" ([HN 50135213](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50135213)). Spam arrives once a listing is large ([HN 40731952](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=40731952)). Readers expect the service to be free ([HN 12401642](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=12401642)), a founder was asked "How do you plan to make money?" ([HN 40751143](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=40751143)), and no working business appeared in any thread.

**First version.** The builder's own city, 30 to 50 sources, a public feed and a weekly email, and a published health page showing which sources broke and when the agent fixed them. No accounts and no tools for organisers.

### 5. A cross-source query gateway for dashboards

**What it is.** A gateway that a dashboard tool such as Metabase sees as one ordinary database, while joins behind it run across several databases and REST APIs exposed as tables. The engine is conventional; the long tail of per-API connectors is written and maintained with a model.

**Evidence of demand.** Metabase's "Join across multiple databases" has been open since December 2016 with **1,797 thumbs-up**; a contributor called it "the most requested feature on our list" in March 2025, and the latest comment is from 5 November 2026 ([metabase#3953](https://github.com/metabase/metabase/issues/3953)). "Driver: REST API", open since April 2017, has 876 thumbs-up; a user asked "some news in 2026?" in January, and a contributor wrote that "paid customer votes count much more than upvotes" ([metabase#4831](https://github.com/metabase/metabase/issues/4831)). A request for an ElasticSearch driver has 1,219 [listing only] ([metabase#1300](https://github.com/metabase/metabase/issues/1300)). These are the highest counts among requests an outsider can deliver. **Where demand is thinner than it looks:** it is one vendor's tracker, the askers are developers and analysts, and a thumbs-up measures agreement, not purchase.

**Closest attempts and why they fall short.** Foreign data wrappers, Trino, Presto, Dremio and DuckDB extensions handle joins; a PostgreSQL HTTP extension and Steampipe handle REST. Commenters find views limited to one server, the HTTP extension "not pretty" and useless off PostgreSQL, and Trino-class tools too much infrastructure to run. In November 2026 an author announced "pgfederate", which runs joins in embedded DuckDB and presents itself as PostgreSQL; by the author's own account it is read-only, single-process, without aggregation pushdown, and serves REST data as a snapshot ([metabase#3953](https://github.com/metabase/metabase/issues/3953)). Whether Metabase's partial in-house work has shipped was not confirmed.

**The obstacle AI removes.** The per-API tail of authentication, paging and schema inference, which a maintainer told users in 2017 to write themselves. The join engine was buildable before capable models, as Trino shows, which is why this candidate only partly passes filter 3.

**Hardest remaining problem.** Performance, since a contributor notes that joining across engines means loading both tables into memory or disk, and trust in a one-person vendor: "Neither my company nor any of the five companies I’ve worked for over the past ten years would ever trust any of their business to a one person SaaS shop" ([HN 46041641](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46041641)).

**First version.** An open-source single binary that speaks the PostgreSQL wire protocol, with two database back-ends, a declarative REST connector format, and five connectors tested against Metabase.

### 6. Translation upkeep for documentation sites

**What it is.** An external orchestrator for documentation tools such as mdBook and Quarto that keeps translated editions in step with the source. When a page changes it retranslates only the changed passages, holds terminology steady, and queues the result for a reviewer who reads the language.

**Evidence of demand.** mdBook's "Add multilingual support" has been open since July 2015 with 182 thumbs-up ([mdBook#5](https://github.com/rust-lang/mdBook/issues/5)); Quarto's "Multilingual websites / books" since January 2022 with 45 comments ([quarto-cli#275](https://github.com/quarto-dev/quarto-cli/issues/275)); SvelteKit's two translation issues have 234 and 145 thumbs-up [listing only] ([kit#553](https://github.com/sveltejs/kit/issues/553)). The class recurs across at least eight tools. **Where demand is thinner than it looks:** counts per issue are moderate, 27 to 234, and the audience is documentation authors.

**Closest attempts and why they fall short.** mdbook-i18n-helpers handles the file plumbing and carries about 18 translations of one course, but each language is in effect a separate book, and adding a language picker meant copying a theme file ([mdBook#5](https://github.com/rust-lang/mdBook/issues/5)). Quarto users rely on a third-party R package or build twice with filters; in July 2026 one reported labels staying in English on French pages ([quarto-cli#275](https://github.com/quarto-dev/quarto-cli/issues/275)). None of the tools in either thread does or maintains the translation itself, and a Quarto team member suggested in January 2026 that an external orchestration tool is the way forward.

**The obstacle AI removes.** The standing human cost of keeping translations current. An mdBook maintainer objected in 2016 to blocking English changes on translators: "I am paid for my work, but others are not" ([mdBook#5](https://github.com/rust-lang/mdBook/issues/5)). Retranslating changed passages on every commit is work a model does cheaply.

**Hardest remaining problem.** Quality that technical readers accept, in front of an audience wary of model output in its toolchain: a suggestion to convert Typst PDFs to HTML with a language model drew 70 thumbs-down ([typst#721](https://github.com/typst/typst/issues/721)). Where AI-assisted contributors have arrived, the bottleneck has moved to maintainers' review time ([mermaid#1227](https://github.com/mermaid-js/mermaid/issues/1227)), so this should ship outside the projects. General translation-management products were not surveyed.

**First version.** A command-line tool for mdBook and Quarto that diffs the source, translates changed segments against a glossary, opens one pull request per language with source and translation side by side, and generates the language switcher.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question. In every case where a model removed the obstacle people named, a second one appeared behind it: reviewer attention in open source, a blind user's trust in software that moves money, the family conversation in estate planning, the insurer's own automation in appeals. The survivors share one design. The model reads and prepares, something outside the model checks (a rules engine, a quoted source passage, a test against the real dashboard, a relative), and a named human sends. An engineer choosing among them is choosing which checker they can build and which human approver the product can count on. The top pick leads because its approver already exists and already wants the job done.

Three cheap tests would move this ranking more than further desk research. Reading where carers and claimants write (Reddit, AgingCare, Benefits and Work, German forums) would show whether candidate 1's demand holds outside programmers and whether candidate 3's European numbers are real. Trying five real errands on five real bank and utility sites with an assisted session would show whether those sites allow it at all. Opening the blocked vendor boards would examine the largest unread pool of repeated, dated, counted demand; a request there with thousands of votes for something an outsider can ship would displace everything ranked here.
